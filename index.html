One mains cycle · averaged 

Average of recent cyclesMains hum (harmonics of the grid frequency)Noise left after averaging

The mic signal is cut into 20 ms slices at the tracked mains frequency, and the slices are stacked. Room noise averages out; the hum repeats every cycle, so it builds up. The yellow curve is the part that repeats at the grid frequency and its multiples. If it rises well outside the grey band, the hum is real.

Camera · rolling-shutter flicker bands 

Frame rate–Band spacing–Row readout–Flicker depth–Exposure–Readout–

The camera reads its sensor one row at a time, so light flickering at 100 Hz lands as bright and dark horizontal bands. Each column here is one frame's row brightness with the still scene removed (gold = brighter, blue = darker). The bands drift between frames because 100 Hz does not divide evenly into the frame rate. That drift carries the flicker's exact phase, which is what gets tracked. Point the camera at a plain wall or ceiling lit by a mains LED or fluorescent lamp, and hold it still.

Each column is one tracking window, rescaled to mains frequency (a 100 Hz hum is halved, a 150 Hz hum divided by 3, and so on). A real mains hum shows as a faint bright line lying along the gold grid line.
